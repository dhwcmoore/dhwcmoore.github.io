Research Profile

Formal methods researcher and systems architect developing machine-checked, executable assurance systems for AI and other consequential software. I work across theorem statements, Rocq/Coq proofs, OCaml extraction and implementation, Python verifiers, adversarial tests, traceability, and trusted-computing-boundary documentation.

My central research question is how an untrusted generator, learned model, or operational system can produce claims whose authority comes from an independent checker rather than from the component that proposed them. Current work covers proof-carrying evidence, authenticated audit manifests, learned observation regimes, structural admissibility, and local-to-global obstruction.

My PhD is in Philosophy. A prior career as a tenured Associate Professor and Director developed research leadership, scientific writing, peer review, curriculum design, and cross-unit programme execution. My formal-methods record is demonstrated in the public proof developments and executable artefacts below.

Creator and Lead Researcher | Rocq/Coq 8.18, OCaml | Active research, 2026

Building a proof-carrying audit system for checking whether a learned observation interface has erased a distinction required by a declared target predicate.

Proof-Carrying Audit Warrants

Creator | Rocq/Coq 8.18, extracted OCaml | Public v0.1.0 candidate

Developed a claim-relative warrant layer for dynamic AI audit evidence, separating a mitigation's effect from the continuing authority of the evidence offered for it.

  • Formalised warranted, defeated, underdetermined, and inadmissible verdicts, with explicit obligations and witnesses.
  • Proved soundness and completeness between a declarative semantics and executable classifier, including preservation and defeat under evidence extension.
  • Built an extracted checker and CLI; 14 public fixtures across three source-gated AI-audit cases agree with the formal decisions. coqchk clean, with no admitted obligations or project axioms.

Regional Obstruction Calculus

Creator | Rocq 9.x, OCaml, Python | Public research development

Developed exact rational and machine-checked methods for detecting and auditing local-to-global coherence failure in finite regional systems.

  • Separates repairability, obstruction, refinement persistence, descent, reflection, and presentation-relative verdict invariance rather than treating them as one claim.
  • Produces constructive repairs or separating witnesses and proof-carrying certificates checked independently with exact arithmetic.
  • The active 37-module Rocq chain is checked with coqc and coqchk; the repository states stronger presentation-invariance claims that remain open.

How I structure an automated decision

1State the claimdefine the requirement, evidence, interfaces, and test
↓
2Generate a candidatean AI model, optimiser, or program proposes an answer
↓
3Check it independentlya separate component recomputes the evidence and tests the claim
↓
4Decide and recordaccept, reject, or defer, and preserve the reason
01

Automation proposes

An automated system should return a candidate that can be inspected, reproduced, tested, and revised. It should not present its own output as sufficient authority.

02

Verification is separate

The component that proposes an answer should not be the only component that checks it. When the evidence is missing or the check cannot be completed, the system should reject or defer the result.

03

Failure produces evidence

A useful rejection identifies the failed condition and the evidence that caused it. A red status light without an explanation is not enough.

Applied prototypes

Private prototypes built from the public work

These projects explore how the public proofs and verifiers could sit beside sensor, infrastructure, and automated decision systems. They are research prototypes, not deployed products.

Private research prototype

VeriBound-FCE

TRL 3

A checker for a proposed sensor-fusion result. VeriBound does not create tracks or combine reports. It checks whether another system’s proposed combination is consistent and supported by evidence.

Operational problem

Two reports may agree whenever they are compared directly, yet the full network of reports may still conflict. A faulty generator may also claim evidence that it did not compute correctly.

Assurance response

VeriBound recomputes the numerical claims, checks certificates with a separate program, rejects altered evidence, and keeps conflicts visible instead of smoothing them away.

  • Command-line checks for direct agreement, whole-network consistency, and evidence assembly
  • Documentation separates working code, tested code not yet connected to the main path, and planned work
  • Designed to sit beside an existing tracking or fusion system, not replace it

Public foundations: the machine-checked mathematics, the certificate and verifier design, and a runnable cyclic-system demonstration.

Private research prototype

STAK-PSAL

ML + verification

An experimental architecture for systems that must reason from incomplete observations. Its full name is the Parallel Structural Assurance Layer.

Operational problem

Each local reading can appear normal while the complete configuration violates a system-wide condition.

Assurance response

A learned model proposes possible structures. A bounded translation step converts each proposal into a finite object. A separate checker then accepts it, rejects it, or asks for more evidence.

  • Python proposes candidates, OCaml performs the bounded translation, and Rocq checks the finite claim
  • stak-psal-toy-v0.1 tests whether time and motion constraints support a proposed event
  • The formal guarantee begins only after the translation into the finite model has been certified

Public foundations: the full structural-admissibility library and a smaller focused Rocq development.

How the parts fit

Let one component propose and another decide

An AI model can search quickly, but it should not approve its own result. The steps below keep the source material, generated candidate, translation, verification, and final decision separate.

Source material Requirements, measurements, code, and models Recorded sources with a known version and origin
→
Candidate generator AI model, optimiser, or ordinary program Proposes an answer and the evidence it says supports that answer
→
Explicit translation Input format, conversion rule, and source record Converts the candidate into checker input and records what changed
→
Independent checker Python or OCaml verifier backed by Rocq proofs Recomputes the claim and returns a result with supporting evidence
→
Release decision Automated gate, stated policy, or human review Accept, reject, defer, or ask for more evidence
A rejected candidate returns the exact failed condition to the engineer or generating system. The checker does not rewrite the candidate and then approve its own repair.
A proposal is not a proof.Generated content remains a candidate until a separate check establishes the claim.
An approximation is not an exact structure.Approximate physical regions must be translated explicitly into the finite interfaces used by the proof.
One-way preservation is not equivalence.A change may carry valid results forward while hiding failures that cannot be recovered in reverse.
A rejection should identify the defect.The result should name the failed condition, the relevant evidence, and what remains unresolved.

What I contribute

          
Clear rules, independent checks, and useful failure reports

          
I build small, testable tools around AI and automated systems. The aim is not to formalise every component. It is to make the critical claim, its evidence, and the decision path explicit.

        

        

          
Clarify the claim
Identify ambiguity, contradiction, missing evidence, and conclusions that do not follow from the stated inputs.

          
Define the interfaces
Specify what enters each component, what it must return, how the result is checked, and where human judgement enters.

          
Record the evidence
Keep the source, version, conversion steps, decision rule, and failure witness so the result can be reviewed later.

          
Build usable tools
Create documented Python packages and command-line programs with automated tests and reproducible examples.

          
Analyse representation changes
State which conclusions survive a conversion and which distinctions the conversion may hide or destroy.

          
Explain the result
Translate specialist work into demonstrations and documentation without overstating what the proof or software establishes.

        

      

    


    

      

        

          
Research and publications

          
One question studied in mathematics, software, and philosophy

          
What must an observation preserve before a system is entitled to make a claim? The manuscripts approach that question through formal mathematics, executable certificates, artificial intelligence, and the philosophy of exactness.

Studies failures caused not by a weak optimiser, but by missing information. The paper develops a formal test and a certificate that records the result.

Exactness Is a Boundary Achievement: Whitehead, Formal Systems, and Placed Concreteness

            
Uses Whitehead to argue that exactness is achieved within stated conditions rather than possessed without qualification. It applies that argument to formal and computational systems, where a proof is exact inside its model but its use still depends on how the model was connected to the world.

Theoretical starting point

          
Why boundaries matter

        

        

          
My philosophical starting point is Whitehead’s view that stable things are produced by organised processes. Region Connection Calculus provides exact terms for how regions connect, overlap, include one another, and share boundaries.

          
These ideas guide the questions but do not prove the results. In the software, approximate physical regions are translated into exact finite interfaces, incidence relations, and compatibility rules. The translation is a separate step and must be checked rather than assumed.

          
The practical rule is simple: keep a conclusion with its source, the conditions under which it was checked, every conversion applied to it, and any assumption that remains unresolved.

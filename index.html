Formal verification · proof-carrying evidence · claim-relative exactness

Imperfect evidence. Exact inference.

Exactness is not a claim that reality is perfectly measured. It is an exact account of what follows from the measurement, model, tolerance, and evidence you actually have. Once those are stated, a nominated claim either follows under those conditions, fails under them, or remains unresolved. I build independent verification that makes that boundary inspectable.

01

Approximation is not ambiguity

"Close enough" has technical meaning only after the tolerance and the claim have been specified. An interval, error bound, or probability distribution is imperfect evidence described precisely. Without the bound, "close enough" is not an engineering criterion.

02

Lost information stays lost

If two admissible states produce the same observation but require different answers, no downstream model can determine which answer is warranted from that observation alone. Optimisation cannot recreate a distinction the input has erased.

03

Local success is not global success

Passing every local check establishes a global claim only when the global claim follows from those checks. Otherwise the global obligation remains unproved. Passing the checks you ran does not prove the check you omitted.

The standard

Exactness is about implication, not omniscience

I do not assume perfect sensors, complete models, deterministic environments, infallible software, or exhaustive knowledge. I ask a narrower and answerable question: given the evidence and conditions actually declared, what conclusion is the system entitled to make? The limits are part of the result.

If a tolerance matters, state it.A tolerance is a parameter of the claim. Leaving it unstated does not make the system practical; it leaves the criterion undefined.
If the observation loses the distinction, stop.No amount of downstream intelligence can warrant different answers from inputs that are identical with respect to the information available.
If the whole matters, check the whole.Pairwise agreement and local success entail a global result only when that implication has itself been established.
If data are transformed, prove what survives.Compression, projection, aggregation, discretisation, and translation may preserve some conclusions and destroy others. Preservation must be stated; equivalence must be earned.
If the generator says it is correct, that is still the generator speaking.Independent assurance requires an independently specified check, not a second assertion from the component whose result is under examination.
If the evidence is insufficient, say insufficient.Underdetermination is not failure to make a decision. It is the correct decision when the evidence does not determine the nominated claim.

The discipline

Do not ask the evidence to support more than it contains

Keep observation, declared uncertainty, transformation, and claim distinct. Then a verifier can separate support from ambiguity, contradiction, missing evidence, and inadmissible evidence. Collapsing those cases into one green or red light throws away information precisely where assurance is supposed to preserve it.

Public code and proofs

One programme, several distinct obligations

The repositories are not separate exercises in mathematical sophistication. Each isolates a place where a system can say more than its evidence warrants. Together they ask whether the evidence can determine the claim, whether compatible states exist, whether the claim is unique, whether local pieces form a whole, whether a representation change preserves the result, and whether the final verdict can be checked independently.

Applied prototypes

The point is not to replace approximation. It is to govern what approximation is allowed to imply.

Real systems fuse noisy measurements, learn from incomplete observations, use thresholds, and trade accuracy against cost. None of that is an objection to exact reasoning. It is the reason the assumptions, transformations, and permitted conclusions have to be explicit.

Private research prototype

VeriBound-FCE

Research prototype

Fusion can produce a cleaner output without proving that its inputs were jointly consistent. VeriBound does not create tracks or combine reports. It checks whether another system's proposed combination is actually supported by the evidence presented for it.

Operational problem

Two reports may agree whenever they are compared directly, yet the full network of reports may still conflict. A generator may also claim evidence that it did not compute correctly.

Assurance response

VeriBound recomputes the numerical claims, checks certificates with a separate program, rejects altered evidence, and keeps conflicts visible instead of converting them into apparent agreement.

  • Command-line checks for direct agreement, whole-network consistency, and evidence assembly
  • Documentation separates working code, tested code not yet connected to the main path, and planned work
  • Designed to sit beside an existing tracking or fusion system, not replace it

Public foundations: the machine-checked mathematics, the certificate and verifier design, and a runnable cyclic-system demonstration.

Private research prototype

STAK-PSAL

ML + verification

A learned model may propose a structure; it does not thereby establish that structure. The Parallel Structural Assurance Layer separates candidate generation from the bounded finite claim that can actually be checked.

Operational problem

Each local reading can appear normal while the complete configuration violates a system-wide condition. The learned model may also be forced to reason from observations that have already lost a distinction the claim requires.

Assurance response

A learned model proposes possible structures. A bounded translation step converts each proposal into a finite object. A separate checker then accepts it, rejects it, or asks for more evidence.

  • Python proposes candidates, OCaml performs the bounded translation, and Rocq checks the finite claim
  • stak-psal-toy-v0.1 tests whether time and motion constraints support a proposed event
  • The formal guarantee begins only after the translation into the finite model has been certified

Public foundations: the full structural-admissibility library and a smaller focused Rocq development.

How the parts fit

Generation and verification are different jobs

A generator can search, predict, optimise, or propose. Its own assertion that the result is correct is not independent evidence for that result. The architecture therefore keeps source material, candidate generation, translation, verification, and release authority separate.

Source material Requirements, measurements, code, and models Recorded sources with a known version, origin, and declared uncertainty
→
Candidate generator AI model, optimiser, or ordinary program Proposes an answer and the evidence it says supports that answer
→
Explicit translation Input format, conversion rule, and source record Converts the candidate into checker input and records exactly what changed
→
Independent checker Python or OCaml verifier, with Rocq proofs for the formal core Recomputes the obligation and returns a verdict with supporting evidence
→
Release decision Automated gate, stated policy, or human review Accept, reject, defer, or request more evidence
A rejected candidate returns the failed condition. The checker does not silently repair the candidate and then count its own repair as evidence that the original proposal was correct.
A proposal is not evidence for itself.Generated content remains a candidate until a separate check establishes the stated obligation.
Approximate measurement is not vague semantics.Uncertainty can be represented explicitly without making the meaning of the claim approximate.
Preservation is not reflection.A transformation may preserve valid results while hiding failures that cannot be reconstructed from the transformed representation.
A failure code is not a diagnosis.A useful rejection identifies the failed condition, the relevant evidence, and what remains unresolved.

What I contribute

          
Reduce assurance claims to obligations that can be checked

          
I do not begin by trying to formalise the world. I begin with the narrower question: what exactly is this system claiming, what evidence would entitle it to that claim, and what would defeat it? The formal work begins at that boundary.

Separate what is asserted from what is merely hoped, approximated, predicted, or assumed.

          
Define the interfaces
Specify what enters each component, what it may conclude, how the result is checked, and where human judgement enters.

          
Record the evidence
Keep the source, version, uncertainty, conversion steps, decision rule, and failure witness so the result can be reviewed later.

          
Build usable tools
Create documented Python packages and command-line programs with automated tests, reproducible examples, and independent checks where the claim requires them.

          
Analyse representation changes

Say what was established, what was not established, and which assumption would have to change for the verdict to change.

        

      

    


    

      

        

          
Research and publications

          
Exactness is claim-relative, not a claim of perfect measurement

          
An exact verdict can be conditional. Given a declared representation, evidence set, uncertainty model, and rule, a nominated claim may follow, fail, remain ambiguous, or lack admissible support. That is not perfectionism. It is refusing to confuse an unstated assumption with an established result.

Theoretical starting point

          
Philosophy supplies the question. Proof must carry the result.

        

        

          
My philosophical starting point is Whitehead's view that stable things are produced by organised processes. Region Connection Calculus provides exact terms for how regions connect, overlap, include one another, and share boundaries.

          
Those ideas motivate questions; they do not discharge proof obligations. In the software, approximate physical regions are translated into exact finite interfaces, incidence relations, and compatibility rules. The translation is therefore an explicit object to be checked, not a similarity to be waved through.

          
The practical rule is severe but simple: preserve the source, state the conditions, record every conversion, keep unresolved assumptions visible, and never strengthen the conclusion beyond what survives those steps.

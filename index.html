Applied assurance systems

From theorem-backed kernels to infrastructure-facing prototypes

These systems connect the formal programme to distributed infrastructure, sensor fusion, defence-relevant coordination, and AI-assisted engineering. They are presented at their actual maturity level rather than as production deployments.

Private prototype

VeriBound-FCE

TRL 3

A proof-oriented structural validation sidecar for proposed fusion results. It checks evidence supplied by another system rather than generating the fusion result itself.

Operational problem

Pairwise sensor or interface agreements can coexist with a globally incoherent result, and a generator can misstate its own evidence.

Assurance response

Separate emitters and independent verifiers recompute exact-rational claims, reject tampering, preserve non-lossy conflict evidence, and assemble pairwise evidence into global diagnostics.

  • Live CLI paths for pairwise checking, global coherence, and evidence assembly
  • Explicit distinction between live, tested-but-unwired, and unimplemented components
  • Designed for integration beside an existing fusion or tracking pipeline

Proposal and prototype line

STAK-PSAL

AR + ML

A Parallel Structural Assurance Layer for high-assurance distributed systems operating under partial observability.

Operational problem

Locally nominal telemetry can conceal globally invalid configurations in command networks, logistics systems, autonomous coordination, telecoms, and industrial control.

Assurance response

A learned model proposes candidate structures; interval-certified projection produces discrete surrogates; a verified adjudication layer accepts, rejects, or defers with a witness.

  • DARPA CLARA TA1 architecture: Python inference, OCaml projection, Rocq-grounded adjudication
  • stak-psal-toy-v0.1 explores velocity-aware admissibility and timed structural constraints
  • No theorem is claimed below the certified projection boundary

AI integration architecture

Put the model inside a governed evidence path

The model is useful because it proposes. It is safe to use in a high-assurance workflow only when proposal, evidence transformation, independent checking, policy, and human authority remain separate.

Inputs Requirements, telemetry, code, models Versioned source material and engineering context
→
Untrusted proposer LLM, optimiser, ML model, generator Produces a candidate plus claimed evidence
→
Typed boundary Schema, projection, provenance adapter Normalises data without silently repairing it
→
Independent authority Python or OCaml verifier, Rocq-backed kernel Recomputes claims and emits a verdict with witnesses
→
Controlled release CI gate, policy layer, human adjudication Accept, reject, defer, or request more evidence
Rejected candidates return a structured witness to the engineering or training loop. The verifier never becomes the generator.
Proposal is not proof.Generated content remains a candidate until independently checked.
Geometry is not algebra.Approximate regions are converted into exact interfaces through an explicit adapter.
Preservation is not reflection.A transformation may preserve accepted results while concealing failures in the reverse direction.
Rejection must be useful.Witnesses identify the failed condition, source evidence, and unresolved obligation.

Theoretical origins

          
Pattern, rupture, and warrant

        

        

          
The wider programme begins from Whitehead’s philosophy of organism: stable objects are achievements of organised process, not inert points that carry their identity without qualification. Region Connection Calculus supplies a disciplined vocabulary for connection, overlap, boundary, and inclusion without requiring point-based geometry as the primary language.

          
That philosophical origin does not substitute for proof. It motivates an engineering discipline: geometric regions and supports may be approximate, representation-dependent, and physically interpreted, while algebraic interfaces, incidence relations, restriction maps, and compatibility conditions must be constructed and checked exactly.

          
For systems assurance, the result is practical. Keep the conclusion, its provenance, its verification conditions, the boundary transformation that produced it, and its unresolved warrant obligations together.
